Gold 15-Minute Breakout Strategy: One Range, One Trade a Day

Gold can spend hours moving inside a tight range before making its next move. Instead of guessing which way it will break, this strategy tracks the overnight high and low, waits for price to cross either boundary, and takes at most one trade for the day. I tested it on the 15-minute gold chart and kept the actual screenshots, including the losing trades.

What makes this breakout strategy different?

Many breakout scripts keep opening new trades whenever price moves above or below a level. This one is deliberately more restrained. It builds one range from the early part of the broker day, places two pending breakout orders, and only allows the first one to become a position. After that, it is finished for the day.

There is also no fixed take-profit target. A trade stays open until its stop-loss is hit or the configured closing time arrives. That makes the rules easy to follow, although it also means a profitable trade can give back part of its move before the time exit.

07:00Start looking for a breakout
1 tradeMaximum per broker day
20:00Close any open position

Those hours use broker time, not necessarily your local time. The default fixed timezone is GMT+3, and the script also has a New York + 7 hours broker clock mode for brokers following that convention.

How a trade is opened and closed

  1. Build the range. Between 00:00 and 07:00, the script records the highest high and lowest low on the chart.
  2. Place both breakout orders. At the end of that window, it prepares a buy stop at the range high and a sell stop at the range low. These are pending orders, not market orders placed simply because the clock reaches 07:00.
  3. Take the first breakout. The first order filled becomes that day's position. The opposite order is cancelled, so the strategy does not reverse and trade back and forth all day.
  4. Exit by stop or time. There is no take-profit. An open position closes at its stop-loss or is scheduled to close at 20:00 broker time. The script runs on confirmed bars, so the close order fills on the next available bar in the TradingView simulation.

It is possible to have no trade on a day when neither boundary is crossed during the trading window. The strategy also skips the first day of loaded chart history because the overnight range may be incomplete.

Risk management is part of the strategy, not an afterthought

The default stop is 1% from the entry price. You can change it to the opposite boundary of the overnight range if you prefer a stop based on market structure. In percentage mode, the script recalculates the stop once using the actual simulated fill price.

Position size can be based on a target percentage of account equity at risk, set to 1% by default, or entered as a fixed quantity. The calculated order size is capped by the maximum leverage setting, 10x by default, and rounded down to the symbol's quantity step. If a position is smaller than one allowed step, the strategy skips it.

The risk amount is a planning estimate, not a guaranteed maximum loss. Gaps, commissions, spread, slippage and execution behavior can increase realized losses. For futures such as Micro Gold (MGC), review the contract's point value, contract step, trading session and the broker's margin requirements before trusting the position size or comparing backtests. The results above were not tested on MGC.

An optional prop-firm-style guard can close positions and halt the day's trading after an estimated daily equity drawdown threshold. It is off by default, acts at bar close, and cannot guarantee compliance with a firm's loss limit.

A dashboard that explains what the script is doing

The chart includes a dashboard so you do not have to guess why a trade did or did not appear. It shows the current session, trend relative to the range, planned stop levels, risk amount, position size, leverage, open P&L, and the running strategy statistics.

Settings worth checking before your first test

Start with the 15-minute chart and regular candles. The range is most accurate when the chart interval divides an hour evenly, such as 5, 10, 15, 30 or 60 minutes. The strategy warns about nonstandard charts or incompatible timeframes but does not automatically disable trades for those warnings.

The most important setting is the broker clock. If the broker's server moves between GMT+2 and GMT+3 with daylight saving time, selecting a fixed GMT+3 value all year can shift your range by an hour. Use the available New York + 7 hours mode when it matches your broker, or enter the correct fixed timezone.

The built-in cumulative P&L panel is optional and off by default. It uses trades available on the loaded chart, so it should not be confused with results from a separate Deep Backtesting range.

Common questions

Does this gold breakout strategy repaint?

The supplied code evaluates its strategy logic on completed bars and submits orders for simulated fills from the next bar. It does not use an intentional future-bar lookahead mechanism. That does not mean all historical and live fills will match, especially with gaps, intrabar volatility or different market data.

Can I use it on XAUUSD or Micro Gold futures?

The script can be added to other compatible symbols, and it contains size-label handling for several gold instruments. The documented backtest is specifically for TVC:GOLD on the 15-minute chart. XAUUSD and MGC have different data, spread, contract and broker-session characteristics, so test them separately.

Why is there no take-profit?

That is part of the setup. The position is allowed to run until the stop-loss or closing time. You can adjust stop placement, but the supplied version does not have a fixed profit target.

Why do my backtest results look different?

Check that the symbol, date range, broker timezone, commission, slippage, contract size and TradingView Strategy Tester settings match. The source test used zero slippage and did not include spread. Even a one-hour session shift can change which range gets traded.
