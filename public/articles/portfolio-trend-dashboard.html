NVIDIA Turned Red Twice. Here’s What the Dashboard Saw.

Four chip stocks were on the watchlist going into Monday, November 2. AMD held yellow. Broadcom and Qualcomm stayed red. Only NVIDIA changed color. It had closed just above its 200-day average on Friday. On Monday, it closed below it. That put NVIDIA at the top of the list of charts worth opening.

By CW · November 22, 2026 · Premium indicator

Monday’s close put NVIDIA on the radar

On October 30, NVIDIA closed at $204.64, just 26 cents above its 200-day simple moving average. It was already below its 50-day average. That combination put the row in yellow. On November 2, the stock opened higher at $207.53, but finished at $202.06. The 200-day average had edged up to $204.46. Once the daily close was available to the indicator, the row would have turned red.

With the status-change alert enabled, the change would show up as NASDAQ:NVDA · YELLOW → RED. The alert is tied to when TradingView makes the latest closed daily candle available to the script. The watchlist also showed two stocks already in red, but those rows had no new alert to investigate.

Tuesday’s bounce was the first test

Once I had opened the chart, the question was whether NVIDIA could get back above its 200-day average and stay there. In this replay, I’d wait for a second close above that line before reconsidering a planned add. On Tuesday, NVIDIA closed at $206.69, above its $204.56 average, and flipped back to yellow. That gave the bounce one confirmed close.

Wednesday was the second test. NVIDIA slipped to $200.13 against a $204.67 average, turning the row red again. On Thursday it closed at $181.60, down 9.3% on the day, on roughly 2.7 times its 20-day average volume. The one-day recovery had already failed before that larger drop arrived.

What waiting changed

By November 20, NVIDIA closed at $125.53, 37.3% below its November 4 close. In this example, the two-close rule would have kept the planned add on hold when Tuesday’s bounce failed. The dashboard helped choose which chart to review. The daily-close rule gave that review a decision you could check the next day.

Set up the same review on your watchlist

Add up to 30 exchange-qualified symbols and give the group a name. The default 50- and 200-day averages define three states. Green means the close is above both. Yellow means it’s above the 200-day average and at or below the 50-day average. Red means it’s at or below the 200-day average.

Enable status-change alerts and create one TradingView alert using Any alert() function call. When a row changes, you can inspect the price, both averages, RSI, MACD and relative volume in the same table before opening the chart.
