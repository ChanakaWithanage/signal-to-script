Four Stocks. One Trend Change. Which Chart Would You Open?

On February 2, 2016, Apple and IBM both showed red. Starbucks was green. Microsoft was yellow. If I had time to open one chart that evening, I’d pick Microsoft. It was the only one of the four that had changed color since the previous close.

By CW · November 22, 2026 · Premium indicator

Why Microsoft was the interesting row

Microsoft closed at 52.62. Its 50-day average was 53.63, and its 200-day average was 47.87. A session earlier, it had closed above both averages. Now the shorter average was overhead while the longer one was still below price. That single change took the row from green to yellow.

Starbucks closed at 60.70, above both 59.72 and 56.31, so it stayed green. Apple and IBM had already closed below their respective 200-day averages the previous session. Their red rows gave me context. Microsoft gave me a reason to open a chart.

How the 30-symbol dashboard reads a watchlist

The Pine indicator puts up to 30 exchange-qualified symbols in one table. Each enabled row uses the last fully closed daily candle. You can keep a 15-minute chart open during the session and still see the same daily trend reading until another daily candle is confirmed.

  • Green means the daily close is above both moving averages.
  • Yellow means the close is above the 200-day average and at or below the 50-day average.
  • Red means the close is at or below the 200-day average. A bounce above the 50-day average alone can still leave the row red.

Each row also shows RSI, MACD and its signal line, and daily volume alongside its percentage difference from the 20-day average. If a row changes color, I can check whether volume picked up before opening the chart. The moving averages and close determine the status color.

One alert for the symbols you follow

I’d start with a group I already review together, such as tech holdings or gold miners. In the indicator settings, name the group, select the exchange and ticker for each holding, and switch off unused slots. For a small watchlist, a handful of rows is enough.

With status-change alerts enabled, create a TradingView alert for the dashboard and choose Any alert() function call. When the script sees a newly confirmed daily value in real time, it reports the symbols that changed and their old and new colors. In the historical example above, the change to investigate would have been MSFT · GREEN → YELLOW.
