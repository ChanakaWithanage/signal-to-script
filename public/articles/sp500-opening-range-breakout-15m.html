SPY Broke Above the Opening Range. Then It Fell Through the Floor.

At 10:00 a.m. on Friday, November 20, SPY was five cents below its opening-range high. It crossed that line, climbed to $718.29, then closed the same 15-minute candle at $713.83. On the next candle, the low of the opening range gave way.

Two candles set the whole plan

For the first half hour of the New York session, the S&P 500 Opening Range Breakout strategy does one job. It records the high and low of the 9:30 and 9:45 candles. On Friday those levels were $717.27 and $711.72. At 10:00, with the range complete, the default setup can place a buy stop at the high and a sell stop at the low.

Friday tested the buy side first. At the range high, a 0.4% stop works out to about $714.40. The breakout candle itself closed below that level. The next candle pushed through $711.72, but the first entry had already canceled the other pending order and used the day’s one trade. If you replay the session, the strategy has a clear answer to the temptation to flip short after a losing long.

The same rule caught a very different morning

Go back one session to Thursday, November 19. The opening range was $735.42 to $739.90. At 10:00 the first break went the other way. SPY dropped through $735.42 and kept moving lower. It stayed below the modeled short stop near $738.36 through the scheduled exit, and the 15:45 candle opened at $716.92.

Thursday shows why the strategy leaves a surviving trade open until the timed exit. Friday shows why it stops after one entry. Together, the sessions give you two concrete behaviors to test: how often the first break carries far enough to hold, and how often it reverses before the next candle.
