Why I built another backtester

When I started testing the Gold Breakout 15M strategy, I kept running into the same problem. A result looks interesting on a few months of data, but what happens over five years? What about ten? Does the strategy make money consistently, or does one unusually good year carry everything?

I wanted to answer those questions without building a new spreadsheet every time. Upload the candles, run the Pine Script, check the trades, then dig into the numbers. That was the starting point for Pine Backtest Studio.

The tool is now public and free to use. It is also open source on GitHub, so the way it works is not hidden behind a subscription page.

More than just net profit and win rate

Two strategies can have the same net profit and very different risks. One might grow steadily. Another might spend months losing money before a few large winners rescue the result. If you only look at the final number, you miss the part that matters.

01 / PERFORMANCENet profit, win rate & profit factor

Start with the familiar numbers and see how many closed trades produced them.

02 / DOWNSIDE RISKDrawdown & recovery to peak

Measure the biggest decline in closed-trade equity and how far the account remains below its previous high.

03 / RISK ADJUSTEDSharpe, Sortino & Calmar

Look beyond returns alone. These measures help compare reward, variability and downside risk, where enough data exists.

04 / TRADE QUALITYExpectancy & average win/loss

Find the typical result per trade, payoff ratio and biggest winning and losing positions.

05 / CONSISTENCYMonthly & weekly P&L

See which periods helped or hurt performance instead of looking at one cumulative total.

06 / BEHAVIORStreaks, trade duration & direction

Compare long and short trades, winning and losing streaks, and average holding time.

You can also export the trade log to CSV, including entries, exits, position sizes, net P&L and modeled fees. That is particularly useful when you want to check unusual trades rather than trust a summary chart.

One of my favorite additions is the difference between realized equity drawdown and the drawdown reported by the backtest engine. The realized calculation tracks the account after trades have closed. The engine can measure equity differently during a position. Seeing both helps explain why risk numbers don't always match.

The analysis also breaks results into weekly and monthly periods, shows longest winning and losing streaks, and separates long-trade results from short-trade results. On the demo screenshot, for example, the latest closed-trade streak is two losses, while the longest winning streak is five trades. Those are the details you can miss when an equity curve looks good at first glance.

Sharpe, Sortino, Calmar and annualized growth may display N/A when the selected period is too short or the underlying calculation is not meaningful. I'd rather show that than make up a precise-looking ratio.

Bring your own historical data, then reuse it

You do not need a special data subscription from us. The backtester accepts ordinary OHLCV CSV files with a timestamp, open, high, low and close. Volume is optional. You can drag and drop multiple files from the same instrument and timeframe, and the app sorts the candles and checks for overlapping data.

In V2, I added a Data Library to make the first step less confusing. There are shortcuts for popular markets including Gold (XAUUSD), Silver (XAGUSD), GBPUSD, EURUSD and NAS100. You can pin favorites, open a provider's data-export page and import your file. The shortcut itself does not download the market history.

You can also save imported candles locally in your browser and load them again in another session. If you prefer to keep a permanent backup, use the built-in CSV export.

What about TradingView? Is this a free alternative?

Yes, if what you need is a free place to run repeated Pine strategy tests on your own historical data. It is useful alongside TradingView, especially for studying periods that are not available in the chart you currently have loaded.

But I don't want to pretend it is a perfect drop-in replacement. TradingView has its own calculation engine, exchange feeds and broker emulator. This project uses the independent PineTS runtime to process the imported candles. Some Pine features are not supported, and simulated order fills or indicator values can differ from TradingView.

There is also an important distinction between the built-in TVC:GOLD reference chart and the backtest feed. The reference chart is display-only. It does not supply its candles to our backtesting engine. If you import Dukascopy XAUUSD data, you are testing Dukascopy XAUUSD candles, not TradingView's calculated TVC:GOLD series.

So I'd describe it as a free TradingView Strategy Tester alternative for independent research, not as an exact reproduction of TradingView's results. Before relying on an unfamiliar script, compare several trades with a trusted reference.

Backtesting costs and position sizing matter too

The setup panel includes instrument point value, minimum quantity, tick size and commission assumptions. There is also an optional Bybit TradFi Gold CFD cost model where you can estimate spread, commission and swap costs using configurable inputs.

This model is for what-if calculations, not a live broker quote. Changing the spread or order size can change your results significantly, especially on small accounts or strategies with many trades.

And no, you do not need to create an account or connect an exchange to use the backtester. It runs in your browser. It does not execute real trades.

Can you really run unlimited backtests for free?

Yes, there is no paid tier, sign-in requirement or built-in quota on the number of runs in this release. You can adjust the script, change the historical range, and run it again as often as your browser allows. Historical data availability and local device resources are the practical limits.

Frequently asked questions

Does Pine Backtest Studio support Pine Script v6?

Yes. It accepts Pine Script v5 and v6 strategy files supported by PineTS. Indicator-only scripts need strategy order logic to produce simulated trades, and not every TradingView feature works in PineTS.

Can I backtest ten years of gold data?

You can test long historical periods if you import suitable, permitted 15-minute gold candles within the 400,000-candle limit. The public site does not bundle a ten-year dataset, and changing the date picker cannot create missing historical candles.

Does the app show Sharpe, Sortino and maximum drawdown?

Yes. It provides risk-adjusted metrics, realized-equity drawdown and the backtest engine's drawdown, along with weekly and monthly performance and trade-by-trade analysis. Some statistics may be unavailable on short samples.

Is it completely free, and do I need TradingView Premium?

Using the backtester is free, with no separate paid plan or backtest-run cap. You do not need TradingView Premium to use this independent application. You do need price data you are permitted to process.

Can I use the results for live trades?

It is a research tool rather than a trading platform. Validate symbol data, contract value, commissions, strategy compatibility and sample trades before making any decisions. Historical and synthetic backtests are not guarantees.
